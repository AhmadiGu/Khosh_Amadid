Before You Go

Check the current advice

Many governments advise against travel to Afghanistan. Read your own government's latest guidance and recent local news before planning anything, and confirm that a place is open and safe to reach. Use this page to learn.

  • Greet with salaam. Place your right hand over your heart when you meet someone.
  • Take off your shoes before you enter a home or a mosque.
  • Say yes to tea. Being offered a cup is a sign of welcome.
  • Ask before you photograph a person. Ask first every time.

The year, month by month

Afghans mark the year with a solar calendar that starts on the spring equinox. It is now year 1405.

Spring

Bahar

بهار

Hamal

حمل

Mar 21 – Apr 20

Nowruz arrives on 1 Hamal. Tulips bloom for the Red Flower Festival in Mazar-i-Sharif and kites go up.

Saur

ثور

Apr 21 – May 21

Orchards blossom on the Shomali plain north of Kabul.

Jawza

جوزا

May 22 – Jun 21

Snowmelt fills the lakes of Band-e-Amir and the roads to Bamiyan open.

Summer

Tabestan

تابستان

Saratan

سرطان

Jun 22 – Jul 22

Mulberries and apricots ripen. Long evenings in the gardens.

Asad

اسد

Jul 23 – Aug 22

Independence Day falls on 28 Asad (19 August) and marks 1919.

Sunbula

سنبله

Aug 23 – Sep 22

Grape harvest around Herat and the Shomali plain.

Autumn

Khazan

خزان

Mizan

میزان

Sep 23 – Oct 22

Pomegranates are picked in Kandahar. Clear, cool days.

Aqrab

عقرب

Oct 23 – Nov 21

Snow reaches the high passes. Buzkashi matches fill the cold months in the north.

Qaws

قوس

Nov 22 – Dec 21

Shab-e Yalda, the longest night, with pomegranates and dried fruit.

Winter

Zemestan

زمستان

Jadi

جدی

Dec 22 – Jan 20

Chilla, the forty coldest days, begins. Families gather indoors.

Dalw

دلو

Jan 21 – Feb 19

Deep snow in Bamiyan and the Hindu Kush. The Salang Pass often slows or closes.

Hut

حوت

Feb 20 – Mar 20

Markets fill with the dried fruits and nuts for haft mewa.
